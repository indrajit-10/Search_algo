01The whole path

Eight stages. The first four decide what to look for, the next two decide which cards, and the last two decide in what order.

02Stages 1–4 · working out what to look for

Four passes over the typed text, each one narrow and each one answering a specific failure.

1 · Normalise

decode %92 and &#nn; → "Mother%92s" → "Mother's" "+" becomes a space → strip accents → lower case apostrophes are DELETED, not split on → "mother's" → "mothers" everything else non-alphanumeric → a space
Why deletion, not splitting. Splitting leaves a stray s that matches almost nothing and drags the whole query to zero. Apostrophes are 51 of the log's 516 distinct queries and 22,411 searches a period — 62% of all search volume, because the upstream rewriter inflects stems into possessives (goodness's, friendship's). Eleven of those queries return nothing at all today: 1,325 searches, 43% of every empty page served.
Deleting the apostrophe is necessary but not sufficient. Applied on its own to the current pipeline it makes matters worse — empty pages go from 3,074 searches to 8,010, because queries that used to fail on a stray s now fail on an unstemmed mothers instead. It only pays off once stemming and the trimmed stop list land with it. See UPGRADE_PATH.md.

2 · Correct the spelling

a word is "correct" if it sits on ≥ 3 cards MIN_DICTIONARY_FREQUENCY otherwise find the nearest word that is bounded Damerau–Levenshtein a swapped pair costs 1, not 2 "birthdya" → "birthday" how many edits are allowed: words < 7 characters 2 MAX_EDIT_DISTANCE words ≥ 7 characters 3 LOOSE_EDIT_DISTANCE but never more than this share of the longer of the two words 0.30 LOOSE_EDIT_RATIO
Why the ratio. Three edits is proportionate on a long word and reckless on a short one. It admits roshasana → roshhashanah (3 of 12 characters) and refuses mariachi → march (3 of 8) — the ceiling of 3 is only reachable at all from ten letters up, because below that 3 edits exceed 30% of the word. Measured on 600 sampled words per band, damaged with three random edits:

One- and two-edit behaviour is untouched: 97.5% and 89.0% under both ceilings. The change only ever adds a third edit, and only to words long enough to afford it.

Why the 3-card floor. The catalogue's own tags contain aniversary, chrismas and birthdy, added years ago as search bait. Without the floor those typos count as real words and never get corrected.

3 · Split run-together words, and link other names

only if the corrector has already given up: "merrychristmas" → "merry" + "christmas" both halves must be words the catalogue uses (≥ 3 cards) separately, 7 validated festival links: deepavali → diwali chanukah → hanukkah dasara → dussehra each checked against the catalogue; 5 of 12 offered were dropped
Why correction runs first. Otherwise aniversary gets carved into ani|versary instead of fixed. Why the links are validated: a claim that two words mean the same thing is a claim about the world, and one of the twelve offered pointed the wrong way round.

4 · Fill the four slots

read from tags and title only — never the description

The single most important rule in the system. A card is funny because someone labelled it funny, not because the word "fun" appears in a sentence about it. 731 cards say "fun" in their blurb without being humour cards; only 200 are genuinely tagged. The description still helps a card be found; it is never allowed to decide what a card is.

03Stages 5–6 · the weight calculation

This is the arithmetic. Worked on a real card, for the real query funny birthday.

The formula

score(card) = Σ over query words idf(word) × placement(word, card) × multiplier + Σ over matched slots slot bonus where placement = Σ over the 5 fields field weight × saturated count saturated = (times × (1.2 + 1)) / (times + 1.2) TF_SATURATION idf = log(1 + (12087 − cards with the word + 0.5) / (cards + 0.5)) slot bonus = 12.0 × min(selectivity / reference, 1.6) FACET_BOOST
the field weights

04Stages 7–8 · freshness and order

Two more operations on that 67.300, and the card has its place.

7 · Freshness

factor = 1 + 0.15 × 0.5 ^ (age in years / 5) RECENCY_BOOST · RECENCY_HALFLIFE boosted = raw score × factor
the curve
Card ageFactor Adds 
new this year1.1500+15.0%the whole allowance
5 years old1.0750+7.5%half of it
10 years old1.0375+3.8%a quarter
20 years old1.0094+0.9%effectively nothing

This card is from 2026 and the newest in the catalogue is 2026, so its age is 0 and it gets the full allowance:

67.300 × 1.1500 = 77.395
Why capped at 15%. The old ranking sorted on lifetime sends, which on a catalogue running since 2002 is a permanent incumbency — a card uploaded last month cannot out-accumulate twenty years of head start. The fix is not to flip that around: a large freshness bonus would recreate the same complaint pointing the other way, with 2026 cards burying a better 2011 match. 15% can reorder cards that were already close and nothing else.

8 · Band, then sort

band = int(10 × boosted / best boosted score in this result set) order = (band descending, tiebreak descending, title) tiebreak = send count when supplied, card year otherwise
Why band at all. Raw scores differ in the third decimal. Compared exactly, the relevance score decides everything and no second sort can ever apply — which is precisely why sends decided almost every result in the old system. Rounding into ten bands is what makes a tiebreak possible.

For this search, funny birthday matched 24 cards. This one scored highest, so it defines the top of the scale and lands in band 10:

int(10 × 77.395 / 77.395) = 10

05When too few cards come back

Stages 5 and 6 run again with less asked of them, accumulating results, until the page holds 20 cards.

the eight rungs, in order
#Ask forGiven up
1every word and every slotnothing
2drop the least informative wordcard, for, my
3the slots plus the strongest wordthe other words
4the slots onlyall free text
5give up the vaguest slot, then the nextrecipient, then tone, then format
6the strongest single word aloneevery slot
7any word at allthe requirement to match all
8the newest cardsthe query — this rung always returns
Occasion is given up last, recipient first. A birthday card that is not mother-specific is still a birthday card; a Mother's Day card is no use to someone who asked for a birthday.

Results accumulate rather than replace. A precise match found on rung 1 stays above anything a later rung adds — the page fills out without the best answer moving.

Rung 8 is unconditional, so zero results cannot happen. The page is told which rung answered, so it can say "we could not find that, here is what is new" rather than passing newest cards off as matches.
