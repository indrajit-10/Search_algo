The search in five ideas

Everything the new search does, and why — five ideas, one worked search, and a table of every word used, so nothing here is jargon. No formulas; those are in LOGIC_FLOW.html if you ever need them.

1

It searches for what they meant, not what they typed

Four repairs happen before anything is looked up. The apostrophe is joined into the word rather than splitting it. Typos are corrected against the words your catalogue actually uses. Words typed run together are pulled apart. Then the query is read as an occasion, a person, a mood and a kind of card — not as loose words.

The apostrophe alone was 96% of every empty page you served — 22,399 searches a period, because love's, friend's and family's each returned nothing.

2

A tag means more than a sentence

A card is funny because somebody labelled it funny — not because the word "fun" turns up somewhere in its description. The description helps a card be found; it is never allowed to decide what a card is.

817 cards say "fun" in their description. Only 200 are tagged as humour. The old search rewrote funny to fun and searched the descriptions — which is exactly why "funny gives wrong results" was reported.

3

Where a word sits decides how much it counts

The same word is worth different amounts depending on which part of the card it is in. A word in the title is deliberate; a word in the description might be a passing mention.

Two smaller rules ride along with it. A rare word counts for more than a common one, so in funny birthday the word "funny" does most of the work. And repeating a word barely helps — saying "funny" five times in the tags is worth 1.8× saying it once, not 5×, so a card cannot win by stuffing.

4

Newness nudges. It never decides

A new card gets at most 15% on top of how well it matches, and that bonus halves every five years — so a card from 2021 gets about 7%, one from 2006 gets almost nothing.

This is the fix for "old cards always first", and the cap is the whole point of it. The old ranking sorted on lifetime sends, which on a catalogue running since 2002 is a permanent head start no new card can catch. A large freshness bonus would just create the same complaint pointing the other way. 15% can reorder two cards that were already close, and nothing else.

5

It cannot come back empty

If too few cards fit, the search gives up the least important part of the request and looks again — keeping everything it already found — and repeats that until the page is full. The last step is unconditional, so an empty page is not possible.

The page is also told which step answered. That is what lets it say "we couldn't find that — here's what's new" honestly, instead of showing popular cards as though they were matches, which is what the old carousel did.

One search, start to finish

Somebody types mom birthdya and hits enter.

tidy
lower case, punctuation out — mom birthdya
correct
birthdya is one swap from birthday. The page says "Showing results for birthdya → birthday".
understand
not two words, but a birthday card for a mother
find
39 cards match both slots
order
best match first, newness breaking near-ties
show
Special Birthday Wishes For Your Mom, then 19 more like it — in about a millisecond

The old search, same query: "Sorry! The search query you entered did not find any matching results. Did you mean mom birthday?" — followed by ten popular cards, of which one was a birthday card and the rest were Raksha Bandhan, Lemon Juice Day, Women's Equality Day and National Dog Day. It knew the right spelling well enough to suggest it, and did not search for it.
